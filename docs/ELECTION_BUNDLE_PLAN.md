# Nationwide Election Bundle Plan

## Goal and boundaries

Produce one versioned, offline-verifiable national election bundle that precinct and canvassing applications can read without network access. A separately signed site assignment selects the device role and jurisdiction. The national bundle contains data and profiles; it does not identify a device or grant an officer authority.

`election-ceremonies` owns the country-neutral bundle format, manifest, signatures, verification, commissioning, assignments, and ceremony pipeline. `election-ph` owns Philippine source adapters, jurisdiction and district resolution, ballot-style compilation, and Philippine coverage rules. `truth-qr` owns QR serialization and its election profile. AES acquires sources, runs the build and review workflow, and consumes the verified bundle. The same bundle must serve precinct and canvassing hosts; assignments and permitted ceremonies distinguish their roles.

## Current baseline (October 2026)

- The pilot ZIP, Ed25519 signature and verifier, read-only SQLite registry, YAML profiles, and commissioning test work. AES still operates through its older preparation and lifecycle services; the pilot bundle is not the live source of truth.
- `resources/election/pop/2025NLE_POP.xlsx` contains 93,629 clustered precinct rows. Existing POP tests assert 93,629 unique IDs and 69,773,653 registered voters.
- `resources/election/clc/2025-national-source-catalog.json` indexes 1,730 official CLC PDF URLs across 18 regional indexes and two national lists. The 1,730 downloaded PDFs and hash manifest are held under ignored `storage/app/comelec-clc-2025/`; they are source material, not a normalized candidate registry.
- The existing AES CLC importer only scans one directory level and copies by basename. It cannot safely ingest this nested national corpus. The current `election-ph` builder accepts already normalized sample arrays and has no coverage report.
- A lifecycle regression involving sample candidate `pres-ada` and a missing compact QR code mapping remains to be fixed before switching the live path.

## Bundle contract

The signed ZIP contains `election.sqlite`, `profiles/ceremonies.yaml`, `profiles/truth-qr.yaml`, a machine-readable source and coverage report, `manifest.json`, and `manifest.sig`. The SQLite registry contains election identity; canonical jurisdictions and district memberships; POP precincts and their jurisdiction; contests and their eligibility scope; candidates and source references; ballot styles; style-to-contest and contest-to-candidate links; and stable style-scoped QR candidate codes. The profile files carry mapping rules, not the candidate or precinct corpus.

The manifest records schema and compiler versions, election and bundle IDs, coverage class, logical registry digest, hashes and sizes of every ZIP entry, and the hashes of the source catalog and POP input. Source provenance records URL or official identifier, local hash, page or row, parser version, and any reviewed correction. No private signing key or entire raw PDF corpus belongs in the distributable ZIP. Keep the raw corpus and build inputs in an auditable authority archive.

All IDs and codes must be stable for a frozen input set and independent of iteration order or SQLite row IDs. Define their derivation and collision checks before importing real candidates. Bundle verification checks the signature against configured trust anchors, every entry hash, SQLite integrity and foreign keys, profile schema, and the declared coverage report before commissioning. Version upgrades must explicitly handle old bundle schemas and reject unsupported ones.

## Philippine geography and district contract

Use an official, frozen Philippine Standard Geographic Code (PSGC) vintage applicable to the 12 May 2025 election. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) maintains PSGC; DILG and COMELEC participate in its interagency geographic-code work. Evaluate the [first-quarter 2025 PSGC masterlist](https://psa.gov.ph/classification/psgc/node/1684076101), record its publication and effective dates, source URL, file hash, and applied name corrections, and justify the final as-of vintage. Never silently substitute a later live PSGC release. Preserve the original POP locality and `PROVINCE` strings alongside normalized PSGC identifiers; the POP field alone does not establish administrative province membership.

Each canonical locality needs a PSGC identifier, type (region, province, city, municipality, barangay), city classification where applicable (component, highly urbanized, or independent component), administrative parent, and nullable administrative province. Record a separate geographic-province association only when useful and sourced. Independent cities attach administratively to the region; NCR cities and Pateros have no province node. The [PSA NCR municipality listing](https://psa.gov.ph/classification/psgc/municipalities/1300000000?vcode=12) identifies Pateros, while [DILG Region VII](https://region7.dilg.gov.ph/lgus/) distinguishes independent Cebu-area cities from their geographic association with Cebu. Do not render an invented province merely to fill a hierarchy level.

Model legislative, provincial-board, councilor, and any parliamentary districts as distinct typed election scopes. District membership needs an election/as-of date, source reference, reviewed status, and the locality or precinct IDs it covers. Resolve 2025 legislative districts using [COMELEC's 2025 elective-positions table](https://www.comelec.gov.ph/php-tpls-attachments/2025NLE/Statistics/12202024_NoOfElectivePositions2025NLE.pdf), district-specific ballot templates, applicable districting laws, and reviewed precinct or barangay mappings. PSGC identifies the locality but does not by itself prove its electoral district. Never expand a ballot-template sample precinct to a whole barangay or city. A locality can have one legislative district and multiple councilor districts; these must remain separate in the registry and UI.

## Implementation sequence

1. **Freeze inputs and provenance.** Verify the POP workbook hash, catalog entries, all 1,730 downloaded file hashes and PDF readability. Record retrieval dates, source index URLs, parser versions, and a source snapshot ID. Compare the official indexes and any later corrections against this snapshot before claiming finality. Preserve missing, changed, duplicate, and superseded sources as review findings.
2. **Normalize the Philippine data.** Add a streaming national CLC import path in `election-ph` that preserves region-relative source IDs and page references. Parse candidates and contests, identify the jurisdiction and district of each, and emit an explicit needs-review queue. Reuse tested AES parsing rules where sound; do not silently accept ambiguous text. Add an equivalent POP adapter or feed from the validated AES POP export.
3. **Reconcile geography and election scopes.** Join POP localities to the frozen PSGC by identifiers or reviewed aliases; classify component and independent cities and province-free NCR localities; preserve every unmatched or ambiguous row. Build a separately sourced 2025 precinct-to-legislative-district crosswalk and typed provincial-board, councilor, and parliamentary district memberships. Review special boundaries and POP corrections before promoting assignments.
4. **Compile ballot styles.** Link every POP precinct to exactly one applicable ballot style, then derive its national and local contests and candidates from the reviewed jurisdiction and district memberships. Resolve naming differences and special cases through versioned mapping data. Generate stable Truth QR codes from the frozen style membership.
5. **Build the national SQLite and evidence report.** Extend the `election-ph` registry schema and builder for streamed nationwide data, source references, and read-only queries needed by both precinct and canvassing hosts. Produce counts and lists of missing or ambiguous PSGC joins, independent-city classifications, administrative province relationships, district memberships, precincts, contests, candidates, mappings, and corrections. Run SQLite integrity and foreign-key checks and verify representative precincts in every region.
6. **Enforce the national coverage gate.** Reject `coverage: national` unless every POP precinct joins to exactly one canonical locality and its eligible legislative scope, or carries a sourced national-only voting exception; independent cities and Pateros have correct administrative parents; every precinct has exactly one valid style; all required contests have the expected candidates and selection limits; all candidate code mappings are unique and reversible; all source hashes match; and the unresolved review count is zero. Include the passing report in the signed bundle. A build with incomplete coverage remains research or pilot and cannot commission for election-day use.
7. **Sign and independently verify.** Build the ZIP reproducibly from frozen inputs. Sign its canonical manifest on an offline authority workstation. Verify it with a separately configured public trust anchor, including corruption, substitution, omitted-file, duplicate-entry, and unsupported-schema tests. Record the final artifact hash and conduct a two-person release review before distribution.
8. **Integrate into AES and canvassing hosts.** Add explicit import/verify/activate commands and make the commissioned read-only registry the source of precinct, ballot-style, candidate-code, and canvassing scope lookups. Fix the `pres-ada` regression first. Keep a pilot fallback during migration, compare outputs against current AES flows, then remove duplicated live mapping logic only after parity tests pass. Test a precinct assigned in one jurisdiction and rejection at another, plus municipal/provincial/national canvassing scope.
9. **Release and rehearse.** Version and publish package changes, lock AES to released versions, build a fresh bundle from the frozen source snapshot, and rehearse offline commissioning, backup-device replacement, key rotation/revocation, and recovery from a rejected or superseded bundle. Publish the artifact hash and public verification material through the election authority's distribution process.

## Election Bundle Workbench in AES

Build a read-only research explorer first, then a reviewed correction workflow. Navigate nationwide → region → [province → component city/municipality, or independent city/Pateros] → barangay → clustered precinct. Skip the province level when the canonical locality has no administrative province. Show the raw POP province label separately from the canonical hierarchy. Keep the bundle ID, source hashes, coverage class, and research or election-ready status visible throughout.

Provide server-side search by clustered precinct ID, PSGC code, locality, polling place, and source reference. Filter by region, province membership (including “No administrative province”), locality type and city classification, legislative district, evidence type, assignment status, and correction state. The precinct detail should show canonical geography, all applicable typed districts, the proposed ballot style, POP row, source URLs and hashes, reviewer decision, and relevant corrections. Aggregate counts should support drilling down and back out without loading all 93,629 rows into the browser.

A correction queue should retain the original publication and proposed change, show the affected precincts and a before/after diff, require a recorded review decision, and preserve superseded evidence. Identified notices remain unapplied until their contents and effective scope are checked. The UI must not turn an unresolved or merely source-supported assignment into a reviewed one by display or bulk action.

## Acceptance tests

- Parser fixtures cover representative PDF layouts in every region, national contests, district variants, duplicates, and malformed pages; every unparsed item reaches the review queue.
- The complete source manifest and POP import can be reproduced from the same frozen inputs, with stable IDs, candidate codes, logical registry digest, and coverage report.
- A national build fails on a removed precinct, omitted CLC source, unmatched district, unresolved candidate, duplicate code, altered profile, or missing trusted key.
- PSGC joins distinguish an administrative province from the raw POP province label and optional geographic association. NCR cities and Pateros have no administrative province; highly urbanized and independent component cities do not inherit provincial election scopes merely from geographic location.
- Legislative, provincial-board, councilor, and parliamentary districts retain distinct types and source references. A district split or ambiguous barangay leaves its precincts unresolved until reviewed.
- Every one of the 93,629 POP precincts resolves to exactly one style and can round-trip its candidate codes through Truth QR. Sampling alone does not satisfy this gate.
- The Workbench's counts, filters, drill-down, and precinct detail agree with the coverage report; a pending correction never appears as applied.
- A verified bundle can commission the same software image for a precinct or canvassing role using different signed assignments; assignments outside scope fail.
- Existing AES role-demo, ballot, return, lifecycle, and pilot-bundle tests pass. Add focused end-to-end tests for bundle activation and actual QR/return behavior before live cutover.

## First delivery slice

Implement the national source snapshot validator and a non-signing coverage report first. It should consume the existing catalog, download manifest, PDFs, and POP workbook, emit deterministic counts and actionable discrepancies, and fail when inputs are missing or changed. This gives the later parser and bundle compiler a trustworthy input boundary without presenting today's corpus as election-ready.

### Implemented research snapshot

`php artisan election:bundle:validate-sources` validates the frozen source inputs and can write a JSON report with `--output`. `php artisan election:bundle:build-research <output> --signing-key=<raw Ed25519 key file>` creates a signed ZIP with a read-only POP precinct and CLC source registry, the two YAML profiles, and the source report. It is explicitly `coverage: source_snapshot` and `readiness: research`. It has no normalized candidates, ballot styles, or candidate codes, so it cannot be commissioned for election-day operation. The subsequent normalization and reconciliation steps above remain required for a nationwide voting bundle.

After transfer, run `php artisan election:bundle:verify <archive> --trusted-key=<raw Ed25519 public key file>` on an offline machine. The trusted key must be obtained independently of the archive.

### 2025 district and correction research

`php artisan election:bundle:reconcile-districts <existing-empty-output-directory>` reads the frozen POP workbook and `resources/election/pop/2025-district-evidence.json`. It writes a precinct-level `district-crosswalk.csv`, a machine-readable `district-reconciliation.json` with every unresolved location, and a `correction-ledger.json`. The evidence file links each supported assignment and correction notice to its source. Direct ballot-template evidence applies only to the printed sample precinct; it is never expanded to a whole barangay. The correction ledger records identified notices as unapplied until their contents and effective scope are reviewed.

The initial pass supports 2,891 of 93,629 POP precincts: 2,488 in eight NCR cities listed with one legislative district in COMELEC's 2025 elective-positions table, 402 in Parañaque through Republic Act No. 9229's barangay boundaries, and one Davao City precinct explicitly printed on COMELEC's first-district ballot template. The remaining 90,738 precincts and all four identified correction notices require review. These outputs have `readiness: research` and cannot establish national voting coverage. Next, freeze the applicable 2025 PSGC masterlist, normalize administrative province and city classification, acquire and hash the official 2025 elective-positions table and district-specific ballot templates, add reviewed municipal or barangay rules, then reconcile every POP row and correction before compiling ballot styles.

### Implemented research ceremony

The Workbench at `/election/bundle` verifies the signed research ZIP and reconciliation hashes. Its review action uses the research-only profile from `election-ph` and the signed assignment, stage checks, idempotency, and journal rules from `election-ceremonies`. AES requires a named reviewer account and stores run events and case decisions in database tables outside the immutable ZIP. A supported precinct district can be approved; unresolved precincts cannot. Correction notices can be deferred or rejected, but cannot be approved until a sourced before-and-after proposal exists.

On the offline authority workstation, create a signed research bundle with `election:bundle:build-research`, then sign its workstation assignment with `election:research:sign-assignment <output-prefix> --signing-key=<raw authority key> --archive=<bundle>`. Transfer the ZIP, public key, assignment JSON, and assignment signature to AES. Configure their paths and workstation ID under `election.bundle_workbench`. Create each named reviewer with `election:research:create-reviewer <name> <email>`; the command prompts for the password without echoing it. The Workbench then starts or resumes one research run for the bundle.

Closing a run writes `decisions.jsonl` and `research-review.json` under the run's review directory. Their digest and unresolved counts remain research evidence. On an offline authority workstation, `election:bundle:build-research <new-output> --signing-key=<raw authority key> --review-report=<research-review.json> --decision-ledger=<decisions.jsonl> --input-bundle=<reviewed signed ZIP>` creates a newly signed bundle containing that evidence. It still cannot be commissioned for election day.
