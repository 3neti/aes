# Alternative Election System
## Operator and Voter Journey
### An Election That Feels More Like an ATM Than an Examination

> **Working Draft**
>
> This document illustrates the intended user experience of the Alternative Election System.
>
> It is intentionally written from the perspective of the people who will actually use the system rather than the engineers who will build it.

---

# 1. Introduction

For decades, elections have conditioned voters to think of voting as a paper exercise.

Find your name.

Receive a ballot.

Shade circles.

Hope the scanner interprets your marks correctly.

This proposal changes that experience completely.

The voter no longer communicates with the machine through pencil marks.

Instead, the voter communicates directly with the computer.

The paper ballot simply becomes the verified record of what the voter already chose.

The experience is intentionally familiar.

Not like filling out an examination.

More like withdrawing money from an ATM.

Simple.

Guided.

Predictable.

---

# 2. Arrival at the Precinct

The voter arrives at the polling place exactly as they do today.

Election officers verify the voter's identity according to existing election procedures.

Nothing changes here.

The election process begins with people—not machines.

---

# 3. Receiving the Tablet

Instead of immediately receiving a paper ballot...

the voter receives a tablet.

The tablet is already configured for the precinct.

It behaves like an appliance.

The voter cannot browse the Internet.

The voter cannot open other applications.

There are no menus.

No settings.

No distractions.

The only thing the tablet knows how to do...

is conduct an election.

---

# 4. Finding a Voting Booth

The voter proceeds to any available table or voting booth.

No special equipment is required.

The booth simply provides privacy.

The tablet replaces the traditional paper ballot during the act of voting.

---

# 5. Activating the Voting Session

The voter activates the voting session.

The screen immediately displays the official ballot.

No login.

No username.

No password.

No complicated instructions.

Only the ballot.

---

# 6. Casting the Vote

The ballot is presented exactly as voters expect.

National offices appear first.

Local offices follow.

Each contest clearly explains how many candidates may be selected.

The voter simply touches the names of the preferred candidates.

Selections are obvious.

Changing a choice is as simple as touching another candidate.

No erasures.

No crossed-out marks.

No accidental double shading.

The system prevents invalid selections before they happen.

---

# 7. Reviewing the Ballot

After every contest has been completed...

the voter reaches a review screen.

This page summarizes every selection.

Nothing has yet become official.

The voter may still return to any contest and make changes.

This review page serves the same purpose as carefully rereading a paper ballot before placing it inside the ballot box.

---

# 8. Finalizing the Vote

When satisfied...

the voter presses:

**Finalize Vote**

At this moment...

the electronic ballot becomes immutable.

The application generates a secure QR Code representing the completed ballot together with the necessary election metadata.

The QR Code does not replace the paper ballot.

It merely carries the information required to reproduce it.

---

# 9. Printing the Official Ballot

The voter proceeds to the Election Officer.

The officer scans the QR Code.

The official ballot is printed immediately.

The printed ballot contains:

- the selected candidates
- precinct information
- ballot identifiers
- a machine-readable QR Code

The voter reads the printed ballot exactly as they would read any important document.

Not the QR Code.

The names.

The voter confirms that every printed choice matches the intended vote.

This is the critical moment.

The paper—not the tablet—becomes the official record.

---

# 10. Depositing the Ballot

If the printed ballot is correct...

the voter deposits it into the ballot box.

Voting is complete.

If the ballot is incorrect...

it is declared spoiled.

The ballot is retained according to election procedures.

A replacement ballot is generated.

The voter votes again.

No incorrect ballot is ever deposited.

---

# 11. Closing the Polls

After the final voter has voted...

Election Officers formally close voting.

No additional ballots may be issued.

Any special polling ballots that must be included in the precinct are received or accounted for.

The Minutes record the legally important facts.

The application transitions to the counting ceremony.

---

# 12. Counting the Votes

The ballot box is opened.

Each ballot is scanned.

Unlike the previous generation of hybrid election systems...

the scanner is not attempting to interpret handwritten marks.

The QR Code already represents the verified selections.

The scanner simply decodes.

Immediately after decoding...

the ballot is displayed on a large monitor visible to election officers, watchers, and observers.

The display shows exactly what the QR Code represents.

Human beings remain part of the process.

Nothing happens invisibly.

If accepted...

the ballot becomes part of the tally.

One accepted ballot.

One append-only counting record.

One step closer to the Election Return.

---

# 13. Generating the Election Return

When every ballot has been counted...

the system generates the Election Return.

The Election Return summarizes the entire precinct.

It is printed.

It may also be represented digitally.

Election Officers review the document.

Appropriate certifications are performed.

The Election Return becomes an official election artifact.

---

# 14. Transmission and Final Backup

After the Election Return is generated...

the precinct performs the required transmission ceremony when the transmission channel is available.

If transmission succeeds...

the transmission evidence is printed or preserved.

If transmission fails...

the failure is recorded and the operator is guided through the approved retry or deferral path.

The appliance then performs final backup.

The goal is not to make the network the source of truth.

The goal is to produce evidence of what happened.

---

# 15. Custody and Closing the Precinct

Once all required reports have been generated...

the precinct is formally closed.

Operational artifacts are preserved.

Certification reports are completed.

Minutes, reports, envelopes, seals, storage devices, and ballot box custody are accounted for.

The election appliance is no longer active.

The election itself continues through the established canvassing process.

---

# 16. The Election Officer Journey

The Election Officer's experience is intentionally different from a traditional administration application.

The application does not present dozens of menus.

Instead...

it guides the officer through a sequence of ceremonies.

The officer always sees:

- the current election stage
- the current ceremony
- the next required action

Nothing more.

The application behaves like a checklist.

Not a management system.

That checklist now includes legal ceremonies such as Final Testing and Sealing, initialization, transmission, final backup, and custody turnover.

Each ceremony still presents only the next required action.

---

# 17. The Voter Experience

The voter should never think about technology.

The voter should simply experience:

- a clear ballot
- simple selections
- an opportunity to review
- a printed ballot
- confidence before depositing it

The technology disappears.

The election remains.

---

# 18. The Design Philosophy

Every screen in the application should answer only one question:

> **What should this person do next?**

Not:

> **What features are available?**

This is the fundamental difference between an election appliance and a conventional business application.

The system guides.

The user follows.

The election progresses naturally.

---

# Epilogue

The Alternative Election System does not attempt to teach voters a new way of voting.

Instead...

it removes the unnecessary complexity introduced by machine-readable paper ballots.

The voter no longer communicates with the computer by shading circles.

The voter communicates directly.

The computer prints the official ballot.

The voter verifies it.

The paper becomes the evidence.

The ballot box remains the source of truth.

And the election becomes both simpler and more trustworthy.
